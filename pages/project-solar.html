Smart Solar-Powered Sensory Anti-Pest Device

For Residential and Office Environments: Enhancing Pest Management

Overview

This project explores the concept of a smart, solar-powered pest management device designed for use in residential and office environments. The core engineering concept combines renewable energy collection through photovoltaic cells with environmental sensor technology to detect and respond to pest activity in an autonomous, chemical-free manner.

The research addresses the growing demand for sustainable, low-maintenance pest control alternatives that minimize chemical usage and reduce manual intervention.

Problem Statement

Conventional pest management solutions often rely on chemical pesticides, mechanical traps requiring frequent human maintenance, or costly professional services. These approaches present problems of safety, cost, environmental impact, and convenience — particularly in office and residential settings where chemical exposure is undesirable.

A solar-powered, sensor-integrated device could provide a sustainable, autonomous, and environmentally responsible alternative for everyday pest control applications.

Research Objectives

  • Investigate the feasibility of using solar energy as a power source for an autonomous pest-detection device.
  • Explore sensor types suitable for detecting pest activity in indoor environments.
  • Identify design requirements for a device suitable for residential and office deployment.
  • Evaluate the concept against existing pest management approaches in terms of sustainability and safety.

Engineering Concepts Explored

  • Solar energy harvesting — Photovoltaic cell selection and sizing for low-power embedded systems.
  • Sensor integration — Motion, infrared, and acoustic sensors for pest detection in enclosed spaces.
  • Embedded control — Microcontroller-based logic for autonomous response triggering.
  • Energy storage — Battery management for continuous operation during low-light periods.
  • Non-chemical deterrence — Ultrasonic, mechanical, or light-based deterrence mechanisms.
